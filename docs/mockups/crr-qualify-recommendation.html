Short answer — yes, and here is the sharper reason

Advance to Stage 3 regardless of score. Your instinct is right, but “encouragement” is not the strongest argument for it. The real one is this: the three actions do not carry the same risk, because they do not carry the same name.

A founder emailing their own investor list is using their own contacts and staking their own reputation. iCapOS is not vouching for anything — so there is nothing for a CRR to protect, and no reason to block it at any score.

Automated Outreach into the platform's investor data, from iCapOS's sending domain, stakes your deliverability and your name. A brokered Introduction stakes it further still — that is iCapOS personally telling an investor this company is worth their time.

So: gate on whose network and whose name, not on which stage the founder has reached.

Why this beats blocking the stage
Block Stage 3 at CRR < 65CRR Qualify (recommended)
The diagnosis The tools that show a founder what investors want — Matching Center, matches, fit signals — live in Stage 3. Blocking them there means telling someone to improve while withholding the instructions. They reach the tools, see the gap themselves, and the score becomes something they can act on.
Momentum A founder stuck at 32 with a wall and a number often just stops. They start emailing their own list — the motion that most often produces the traction and LOIs the score is missing.
Your name Protected. Equally protected. Nothing that sends from iCapOS or carries your endorsement moves an inch.
What the score means “Permission to exist on the platform.” “Permission to use our network.” Narrower, and far easier to defend to a founder who disagrees with their score.
The real risk in this design, named plainly. A score that gates nothing gets ignored. If “Qualified” only unlocks things founders do not want, the CRR becomes decoration and the whole engine stops mattering. This survives that test only because access to your investor network is the single thing founders most want from iCapOS — so the gate keeps its teeth exactly where the teeth are worth having. If you ever find founders shrugging at Qualified, that is the signal the tiering has drifted, not that the score needs to be stricter.
One rename, and it does real work. “CRR 32/100 · gate 65 · 33 short” reads as a verdict on the company. Building → Qualified reads as a path with a door at the end. Same number, same threshold, and the founder now knows what the door opens rather than only that it is shut.

Where I would still hold the line. Brokered introductions should need CRR ≥ 65 and a person — not because the score is untrustworthy, but because an introduction is the one action where you cannot take it back. The founder can send a bad email to their own contact and only they pay for it; an introduction you made badly costs you an investor relationship.

Two things worth deciding alongside this. Whether “Qualified” should notify the founder the moment they cross it — the crossing is the most motivating moment in the whole journey and currently nothing marks it. And whether dropping back below 65 should revoke access mid-campaign, or only prevent starting a new one; revoking mid-flight is defensible but will feel arbitrary unless the founder was warned as the score fell.
