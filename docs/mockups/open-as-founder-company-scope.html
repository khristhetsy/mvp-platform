Open as founder lands on the wrong company

Founder's Preparation menu · traced through the code · mockup

The cause — one missing value

The button never sends the company you are looking at. OpenFounderItem posts only { founderId } to /api/admin/act-on-behalf. The company id in the URL you are standing on — /admin/companies/0c716114-… — is dropped on the floor.

The acting session then has to guess, and the guess is hard-coded: resolveActingFounderScope ends with .order("created_at", { ascending: true }).limit(1) — the founder's OLDEST company. For any founder with more than one company row, every one of the nine Preparation links opens a different company than the one on your screen.

The platform explicitly supports several companies per founder — orgs, Deal Companies, second orgs — and getActiveCompanyForUser exists precisely to pick between them. The act-on-behalf resolver ignores all of it.

Proposed

Acting as You are viewing as Martin Goldberg · Tinski Tech · 1 of 2 companies

The company count only appears when the founder has more than one — otherwise it is noise.

And the silent bounce, which hides the failure

When resolveActingFounderScope returns null for any reason — no company row, an expired cookie, a missing signing secret — the page falls through to requireRole(["founder"]), which redirects staff to /admin/dashboard. No message, no explanation. From your side that is indistinguishable from the link being broken, which is part of why this was hard to pin down.

⚠ Proposed: the founder page shows an explicit acting-as failure instead of bouncing — “Couldn't open as Martin Goldberg: this company isn't linked to that founder.” with a link back to the company workspace. A redirect that eats the reason is how a configuration problem looks exactly like a bug.

Why the ownership check has to stay server-side. The company id now travels in a cookie, and a cookie is something the holder can try to edit. The signature stops tampering, but the resolver should still prove the company belongs to that founder on every read — the same posture the module already takes with the staff permission, which it re-checks on every request rather than trusting the token.

Why not read the company from the referrer. It would work for this button and fail for every other entry point, and a referrer is not something to make an authorization decision on. The company belongs in the session because the session is what the founder pages read.

One thing worth deciding. Whether an acting session should be allowed to switch company mid-session, or whether Stop-and-reopen is safer. Switching is more convenient; reopening makes every acting session traceable to a single company in the audit trail, which is easier to defend later.
