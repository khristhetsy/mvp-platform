Revised plan. The previous mockup proposed collapsing the two layers into per-stage factor points. I talked myself out of it — that breaks the stage-neutral total the outreach gate hangs on, and it makes the real defect worse. This version fixes the two things that are actually wrong and puts the per-stage recommendation where it belongs.

1. The two layers, explained on screen — they aren’t rivals, nothing says so

Factor points and profile weights answer different questions. Nothing on the tab says which is which, so “Narrative 4” sitting near “Narrative 30” reads as a bug. One explanatory line each, and a dimension table that shows both numbers side by side instead of only one.

Granularity is the new column, and it is the reason for section 2. It shows how many distinct values a dimension can take. A dimension carrying 30% of the score should not move in quarters.

2. Split Narrative and Team — the defect that actually matters

Narrative is one factor with a maximum of 4. So the Narrative dimension can only ever be 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100 — and at pre-seed it carries about a third of the score. One yes/no-ish judgement on the deck swings a pre-seed founder’s rating by 7½ points at a time. Team has the same shape: one factor, one question, 30% of the weight.

Splitting Narrative into three factors (section 2) will lower those 100s on its own, since a company with a good deck and no written plan can no longer score full marks. Do that first, re-score, then look at this card again — the recommendation may shrink once the evidence is finer.

This is a judgement, not a benchmark. There is no published industry standard for these weights — the thirteen (soon sixteen) factors are iCapOS’s own rubric, so there is nothing external to compare against. What the card can honestly do is reason about your scored companies: which dimensions are flat across everyone, which spread the field. The screen says so in those words rather than claiming an industry norm.
The benchmark that will be real, later. ir_matches already records how far each investor got — matched → intro_sent → contacted → meeting_scheduled → meeting_held → committed / passed. With enough of those, the card gains a second, defensible source: “from your own results — which factors separated the companies that got meetings.” Worth shaping the question now so the data is usable when the volume arrives.

4. What it costs, and the one hazard

Small

  • The explanatory lines and the Raw-share / Granularity columns — display only, the arithmetic exists.
  • The recommendation card and Apply — loads numbers into the editor, no new save path, no migration. Profile weights are already per stage and already versioned.
  • The “flat across everyone” reasoning is a read over the scores you already store.

The hazard — splitting factors needs a re-score

  • New factor keys mean old score rows have no value for them. The rollup skips factors with no stored score, so an un-re-scored company would read Narrative 0, not “unchanged”.
  • Fix: a compatibility map — until a company is re-scored, the old pitch_quality ratio stands in for the three new narrative factors, and founder_team for the two team ones. Old rows keep reading correctly; new rows use the real split.
  • Then Re-score All, which already exists. 22 companies.
  • The AI scorer needs the two new questions written — business-plan depth and team gaps — and a use-of-funds check moved out of Deal structure.

Suggested order: (1) the explanation and the two new columns — zero risk, and it makes the rest legible. (2) the recommendation card — no migration. (3) the factor split with the compatibility map and a re-score. Each is useful on its own; you can stop after any of them.
