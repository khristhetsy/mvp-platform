What changes from what we have. The Weights tab keeps everything it does today — the profile tabs, the factor points, the dimension weights, the bands, the floors, Preview impact, Save, History. One section goes above them and changes what you look at first: whether the rubric is separating your companies at all. Read the shape, not the rows. 63 of the 100 available points sit in factors that more than half your companies score zero on. The rubric is currently measuring, more than anything, whether a company has uploaded documents. That is a legitimate thing to measure — but it is not what the weight editor is for, and no weighting change fixes it.
What this section is for. Not to change anything automatically. To tell you which of three different problems you are looking at before you edit a number: floor (the evidence is not in the data room — a founder-chasing problem), ceiling (the question is too easy — a rubric problem), no variance (the factor is dead weight — delete it or make it harder).

Raw share — how many of the 100 engine points live here. Read-only; it is the sum of the factor points.
Weight — how much a pre-seed reader should care about the dimension once scored. This is the number you edit.

These weights are a stated judgement. There is no industry standard for them — these factors are iCapOS’s own rubric, so there is nothing external to benchmark against. Record why you set them in the save reason; that is what the History tab is for.

What I am NOT proposing any more, and why. I previously offered an AI card that recommends weight numbers per stage. I would drop the numbers and keep the diagnosis. The established finding is that with no outcome data, elaborate weights do not beat simple ones — so a generated set of twenty weights adds precision you cannot cash, dressed up with authority it has not earned. The useful half of that card is already above, as the Discrimination column.

One line, always visible. It stops the tab implying an accuracy nobody has established.

What would make it measurable

  • ir_matches already records how far each investor got: matched → intro_sent → contacted → meeting_scheduled → meeting_held → committed / passed.
  • Once there is volume, the question is simple: did companies with higher CRR get more meetings? and which factors separated them?
  • That is the point at which weights stop being opinion and get fitted — and the point at which this panel gains a real number.
  • With 22 companies and no recorded outcomes yet, anything computed today is directional at best. Worth instrumenting now, not analysing now.
