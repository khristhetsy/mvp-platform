Why it looks like that

Rows of three in the same columns block the companies use — real table cells, so Outlook lays them out. No avatars: an email client that blocks images would leave a row of broken boxes, and initials in a circle cost a CSS trick that Outlook ignores.

Two rows per group, then a count. Forty-one names would add a screen and a half to an email whose job is to get one click. The full list lives on the event page, which the CTA already goes to.

It sits below Presenting companies and above the closing CTA — the last thing read before the button, and the most persuasive: who else is in the room.

Its own tick beside Who’s presenting, so a day-of email can drop it while keeping the companies.

The tick that makes the left-hand version legal

Networking

Shown to investors and founders only, and it goes in the registration field set — so it is editable in Event Hub → Registration fields like everything else on that form.

Worth knowing

The email can’t ask who is reading it. There is no session behind an email client, so the email shows the public list only — the people who ticked the box. An email to registrants naming everyone would leak the private ones to anyone the mail is forwarded to.

The 41 already registered never saw that tick. They default to private and stay a number until they opt in. So on the day this ships the public list may be very short — that is correct, not broken.

Badges come from attendee_type, chosen by the registrant. Nothing verifies that someone who picked “Investor” is one, and a public badge reads as an endorsement. Worth deciding whether staff should be able to correct a badge before it shows.
