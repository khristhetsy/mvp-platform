✦ Recommended for Pre-seed

At pre-seed there is usually no revenue and no cohort data to read, so a rubric that puts 15 points on Revenue is mostly measuring whether a company is later-stage, not whether it is ready. Weight moves to what does exist at this stage: who the founders are, whether the story holds up, and whether the market claim is evidenced.

  • Founder & Team 11 → 22. The most-cited kill reason pre-seed.
  • Pitch & plan 4 → 14. At this stage the deck is the evidence.
  • Revenue 15 → 6. Scoring absent revenue punishes every pre-seed company equally, which tells you nothing.
  • Exit strategy 7 → 3. An exit plan written pre-seed is a guess.

Loads the numbers into the editor. Nothing is saved until you save a version, and Preview impact still shows you who moves first.

Narrative is one factor carrying 14 points. Giving the story real weight at pre-seed means one question — “is the deck any good?” — decides 14% of the score on its own. If that is the intent, fine. If not, Narrative needs splitting into two or three factors before it can hold that much. Worth deciding before you apply this.

Read strictly, “dimensions reflect the factor points and are not adjustable” could mean: keep one set of thirteen maxima for everybody, derive the five dimensions from them, and delete the four profiles. Simpler — but it throws away stage sensitivity entirely.

4. “Industry norm” — the honest answer about where a recommended number can come from

You asked for industry norms or AI recommendations. Those are not the same thing, and only one of them is available today. This matters because a number labelled “industry norm” carries authority it may not have earned.

SourceAvailable?What it really is
A published industry norm no There is no public dataset of readiness-factor weights by stage. These thirteen factors are iCapOS’s own rubric — nobody else scores against them, so there is nothing to benchmark against. Labelling anything here “industry standard” would be inventing authority. This is the same rule the AI guardrails already enforce: no fabricated statistics.
AI judgement, labelled as judgement yes, today Claude reasons from what is generally understood about what investors weigh at each stage, and — usefully — from your own scored companies: which factors are flat across everyone at a stage (measuring nothing), which spread the field. Presented as “Recommended for Pre-seed” with its reasoning shown, never as a benchmark.
Derived from iCapOS outcomes not yet The real prize. ir_matches already records the stage each investor reached — matched → intro_sent → contacted → meeting_scheduled → meeting_held → committed / passed. Once enough of those exist, you can ask which factors actually separated the companies that got meetings, and set the weights from that. That is a genuine, defensible benchmark — and it is yours, not the industry’s. Needs volume first; worth designing the question now so the data is there when it is.
Suggested wording on screen: “Recommended for Pre-seed” with the reasoning visible and an explicit “This is a judgement, not a measured benchmark” line — the same honesty the score panel already uses. When outcome data can support it, the same card gains a second source: “From your own results — 43 investor outcomes across 9 pre-seed companies.”

5. What this costs

Straightforward

  • Dimension row becomes read-only — delete the inputs, print the sum. The arithmetic (rollupWith) already exists.
  • The recommendation card, and Apply loading numbers into the editor. No new save path.
  • Because factor points are stored as data already, changing them re-scores without re-running the AI — each factor contributes its ratio, which is why the CRR weights build worked.

Needs care

  • Factor points become per-stage. Today crr_weight_sets.factors is one object; it becomes four. That is a migration and a change to scoreColumnsFor, which currently rolls up once and weights four ways — it would roll up four times instead.
  • Every stored score shifts the first time you save. Preview impact will show exactly who, before you commit.
  • Narrative at 14+ points rests on one question. Decide whether to split it first.
  • The profile weight table disappears. Its job moves into the per-stage points. Saved versions keep their old shape, so History stays readable.

Open question for you: do factor points go per-stage (section 2), or stay shared with the profile weights kept as they are? Everything else follows from that answer.
