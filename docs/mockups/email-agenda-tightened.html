Four changes

ChangeRuleWhy
Abstracts are trimmed First sentence, or 120 characters, whichever is shorter An agenda tells you what’s on. The full abstract already lives on the event page, one click away, and it reads better there
One session leads The talk show, or the keynote when there isn’t one Everything at the same weight means nothing stands out. The lead session gets the tint and the guest billing; the rest become rows
Rows, not cards Badge in a fixed column, title, one line A vertical line of badges scans in a second. Four boxed cards do not
Exhibits collapse to one row Sessions whose title starts “Exhibitors -” merge, naming the companies They’re booths, not sessions — see below

The part the email can’t fix

“Exhibitors - NAI Technology” is a session typed founder_showcase. That is why it carries a FOUNDER SHOWCASE badge and a company’s marketing paragraph in the agenda. The merge above is a patch over the mislabel, not a repair of it.

The repair is to delete those two sessions and add NAI Technology and Impervitex to the roster with role Exhibitor. They then appear on the Exhibitors line, out of the agenda entirely, and the patch becomes unnecessary. Your call which one you want — I’d do both, since other events will have the same sessions already.

Worth knowing

Trimming is display-only. Nothing is written back to the session — the abstract stays whole on the event page and in the booklet, which has room for it.

A first sentence can still be long. The NAI paragraph’s first sentence is 27 words, so the 120-character cap is what actually bites there. Cut with an ellipsis, never mid-word.

The Auditorium abstract starts with “Auditorium:” — the title repeated inside its own description. Trimming hides it; editing the session removes it.
