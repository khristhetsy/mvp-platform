The invitation, and the chaser

Subject: Marcus Reyes would be worth meeting at Las Vegas · Edit
Hi {{first_name}},

You both opted into networking at {{event_title}}, and you share {{shared_interests}}.

{{match_name}} — {{match_company}}, {{match_badge}}.

If you’d like an introduction, accept below. Nothing is shared until you both agree — no contact details change hands before that.
Accept the introduction →
Not interested? Decline, and they won’t be told who declined.

The chaser is the same message with one line changed and is edited on its own tab. Both are blocks in the Marketing editor, like the event emails — so the compliance footer is appended at send and can’t be removed.

Chasing

Chase after 3 days with no answer
Chase again after 3 more, then stop
Never chase a declined match
Stop everything 24h before the event starts
Chase the other side too when neither has answered

Two chasers maximum. A third is not persistence, it is the reason people mark mail as spam.

Before you decide

None of page 3 exists today — including the invitation. Event networking currently works one way only: an attendee opts in, sees a list of names suggested to them, clicks Connect, and the other person accepts or declines in the app. Staff never see any of it, nothing is emailed, and there is no message to edit because no message is ever sent.

So this is a new feature, not a view over existing data. It needs: matches stored rather than recomputed per page load (today they exist only for the split second a suggestion list renders), an invitation record with sent/accepted/declined and a chase count, an editable template, and a daily job to send the chasers. The matching itself already works and would not change.

Staff-initiated invitations change what attendees agreed to. Today a request comes from another attendee who chose to send it. An emailed invitation from iCapOS saying “you two should meet” is us making the introduction — worth being sure that is the product you want, because it reads very differently to the person receiving it.

The score is thin. Shared sectors ×2, plus 3 when one is a founder and the other an investor — no stage, no cheque size, no geography. It ranks a suggestion list adequately; it is a weak basis for an email asserting someone is worth meeting. Why matched is on the table so staff can see exactly how thin before sending.

“Automatically” needs a boundary. The chasers above are capped at two and stop a day before the event. Without both caps, a busy event sends a few hundred unsolicited emails to people who have already ignored one.
