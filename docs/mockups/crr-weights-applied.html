1. How factor points can “reflect” four different weightings

There is one set of thirteen factor maxima and four sets of weights, so the raw share cannot equal all four columns at once. It can equal exactly one thing coherently: the average of the four. That is the right answer, because total_score — the raw 0–100 the factor points produce — is the stage-neutral number, and it is what the 65 outreach gate reads. A stage-neutral total should be shaped by the stage-neutral average.

A useful side-effect. Narrative goes from 5 possible values to 14, and Team from 12 to 24. The granularity problem — a dimension carrying 30% of the pre-seed score while moving in quarters — largely solves itself, without splitting any factors.
And the reason I would NOT type the factor points in yet. 14 of 22 companies already max out Pitch Deck at 4/4. Raising it to 13 hands roughly nine free points to two-thirds of your list — the average would jump from 17 to somewhere near 26 with nothing having improved. That is score inflation, and it makes the 65 outreach gate mean less, not more.

The fix is the one from the discrimination mockup: split Narrative into three harder questions first (deck · business plan · raise & use of funds), so 13 points has to be earned rather than handed over. Then these factor points are safe.
Second, smaller flag: Governance drops 9 → 6, and it is currently the widest-spread factor you have — the one doing the most real discriminating. Following the average mechanically demotes your best signal. Worth overriding to 8 and taking the 2 points off IP, if you want to protect it.
